Anthurium bakeri Care: The Strap-Leaf With Red Berries

Anthurium

A tough, pendant Central American strap-leaf that rewards patient growers with a spadix studded in scarlet berries.

The picks
01
Anthurium bakeri
Epiphyte · strap-leaf, pendant

Native from Guatemala south through Costa Rica and Panama, A. bakeri throws narrow, leathery leaves 18–28 inches long with reddish-brown speckling on the underside. It's one of the easiest strap-leaves to flower indoors, and the infructescence ripens into a tight cluster of bright red berries that hold for weeks. Mount it or grow it in a slatted basket so the inflorescence can hang. Forgiving of brief humidity dips that would scorch a velvet.

Common questions

How do you tell Anthurium bakeri from friedrichsthalii?
Check the berry color and leaf underside. A. bakeri has reddish-brown speckling on the underside of the leaf and produces bright red berries; friedrichsthalii has a clean green underside and white to pale lavender berries. Leaf shape overlaps enough that vegetative ID alone is unreliable, so wait for fruit if you're buying an unflowered plant.
Why won't my Anthurium bakeri flower?
Almost always insufficient light. Bakeri needs sustained bright indirect light — roughly 150–250 µmol PPFD for 12 hours — to initiate inflorescences, and a plant in a dim corner can go years without blooming. Move it closer to a window or add a grow light and expect spathes within six to nine months.
Are the red berries on Anthurium bakeri toxic?
Yes, like all parts of the plant they contain calcium oxalate crystals and are not safe to eat. The berries cause mouth and throat irritation in humans and pets if chewed. The seeds inside are viable for propagation, though, and can be sown on damp sphagnum after rinsing off the pulp.
Can Anthurium bakeri be grown mounted?
Yes, and it does well mounted on cork or tree fern with a thick sphagnum pad. The tradeoff is daily watering — mounted strap-leaves dry fast, and bakeri will brown at the leaf tips if it desiccates between soakings. Most growers find a slatted basket gives the same airflow benefits with less maintenance.
How fast does Anthurium bakeri grow?
Moderately, by anthurium standards. Expect three to five new leaves per year on a healthy adult, with each leaf reaching full size in six to eight weeks. Flowering plants slow leaf production while berries develop, which is normal and not a sign of stress.
