Anthurium jenmanii
Epiphytic rosette · thick strap leaves

The species that built the bird's-nest market. Wild jenmanii from the Guianas and northern Brazil produces stiff, paddle-shaped leaves with a slight wave at the margin and a pronounced midrib. Indonesian growers have run it through decades of selection, so most plants in circulation are seed-grown selections rather than wild collected. Forgiving of warm nights and tolerant of brighter light than most velvets.

Common questions

Is Anthurium jenmanii the same as Anthurium hookeri?
No — they are distinct species frequently confused in trade. True hookeri has tiny black glandular dots along the leaf margin and a narrower, more tapered blade; jenmanii has a clean margin and a broader, more paddle-shaped leaf with a heavier midrib. Always ask a seller for a close-up of the leaf edge before paying a premium for either.
Why is my jenmanii producing thin, floppy leaves?
Almost always insufficient light. Adult jenmanii leaves develop their characteristic thickness and ribbing only under bright indirect light — roughly an east-facing window with sheer or 200–400 µmol/m²/s under lights. Underfeeding contributes, but light is the primary variable; a plant moved into stronger light will throw thicker leaves within two or three growth cycles.
Can Anthurium jenmanii be grown mounted?
Yes, mature plants do well on cork rounds or tree-fern slabs with a sphagnum pad at the root mass. Mounted culture suits the species' epiphytic habit and largely eliminates crown rot risk. Expect to water daily in summer and provide humidity above 60%, since a mount dries far faster than a pot.
Are the named Indonesian jenmanii cultivars stable from seed?
The well-established lines like 'Cobra' and 'Big Wave' are reasonably stable when both parents are selected, but seed-grown plants always show variation. Buying a seedling labeled 'Cobra' gets you a strong probability of the hooded leaf form, not a guarantee. For a true-to-type plant, buy a division or a mature specimen showing the trait in adult leaves.
How fast does Anthurium jenmanii grow?
Moderately — expect three to five new leaves per year on a healthy adult plant in good conditions. Seedlings can push faster while in juvenile form but slow down as they transition to adult leaves around the third or fourth year. Warm nights (above 65°F) and consistent feeding are the biggest accelerators.
