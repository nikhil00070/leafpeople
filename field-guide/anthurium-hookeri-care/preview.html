Anthurium hookeri and the Bird's-Nest Form

Anthurium
100+ leaves in a mature rosette

The rosette anthuriums are quieter than the velvets, but the good ones reward a grower who reads them carefully.

Light
Bright indirect to dappled sun; tolerates more than velvets
Water
Top inch dry between waterings; never into the crown
Humidity
65%+ ideal for true hookeri; 50% workable
Substrate
Chunky: bark, pumice, coco husk; no peat
Native range
Lesser Antilles, Venezuela, Guyana, northern Brazil
Mature size
Rosette to 1.5 m wide, leaves 60–90 cm

01
Anthurium hookeri
Epiphytic rosette · paddle leaves

The type species for the bird's-nest section. True hookeri has spatulate leaves with conspicuous black marginal glands along the edge and a stubby, often purple-tinged spadix that ripens to white berries. Wild plants form vase-like rosettes on trees throughout the Lesser Antilles and northern South America, catching leaf litter as a private compost heap. Most plants sold as hookeri in the trade are not — check for those black glands.

Common questions

How do I tell true Anthurium hookeri from lookalikes?
Look for black glandular dots along the leaf margin — they are diagnostic for hookeri and absent in nearly every plant sold under the name. The leaves are also spatulate (wider toward the tip) with a rounded apex, and the spadix is short and stubby compared to the long spike of schlechtendalii. If a seller can't confirm the marginal glands from a photo, assume it's a hybrid or jenmanii.
Can Anthurium hookeri grow as a houseplant without a greenhouse?
Yes, but it will be slower and fussier than schlechtendalii or plowmanii under the same conditions. Hookeri resents humidity below 55% and tends to throw smaller, slightly distorted leaves in dry air. A grow tent, IKEA cabinet conversion, or a humid bathroom with good light will get you cleaner growth than open-room culture.
Why is water pooling in the rosette a problem if these plants catch leaf litter in the wild?
In habitat, constant airflow and warm sun dry the funnel between rain events, and the trapped debris is mostly dry leaves rather than standing water. Indoors, still air plus pooled water creates anaerobic conditions that bacterial pathogens like Erwinia exploit within days. Empty the crown after watering or misting, especially in cabinets.
How fast do bird's-nest anthuriums grow?
Slowly — expect four to eight new leaves per year on a mature plant in good conditions. They put a lot of energy into root mass and individual leaf size rather than leaf count. A seedling plowmanii takes roughly five to seven years to reach the dramatic funnel form people buy them for.
Are these plants safe around pets?
No. Like all anthuriums, the bird's-nest species contain calcium oxalate raphides that cause oral irritation, drooling, and swelling if chewed. They aren't typically fatal but can be miserable for cats and dogs. Keep them out of reach, particularly the lower leaves of large rosettes that hang at pet height.
