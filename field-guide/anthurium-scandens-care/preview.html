Anthurium scandens, the pearl-berry epiphyte

Anthurium
1,000+ Anthurium species described

A scrambling neotropical epiphyte grown less for foliage than for the milky, almost luminous berries it carries along its stem.

Light
Bright indirect, no direct sun
Water
When mix nears dry; rainwater or RO

70%+ to fruit; tolerates 50%
Difficulty

The picks
01
Anthurium scandens subsp. scandens
Epiphytic climber · narrow lance leaves

The wide-ranging form, found from southern Mexico down through Brazil and the Antilles. Leaves are lanceolate to elliptic, 6–15 cm long, leathery, and a flat mid-green. The reward is the infructescence: short spadices that swell into clusters of pale, translucent berries reading nearly white in good light. It climbs willingly on bark slabs and tolerates more neglect than most Anthurium.

Common questions

Are the white berries of Anthurium scandens edible?
No. The berries of Anthurium scandens contain calcium oxalate crystals like the rest of the plant and will cause oral and digestive irritation if eaten. They're ornamental only. Keep them away from curious pets and children, and wash your hands after handling crushed fruit.
Why won't my Anthurium scandens flower or fruit?
Almost always insufficient light or humidity. The plant produces spadices reliably under bright diffuse light around 200 µmol/m²/s with humidity above 65%, and fruit set improves further above 70%. If light and humidity are good but you still get no berries, the plant may simply be too young — wait until the main stem is 20 cm or more with several mature leaves.
Does Anthurium scandens self-pollinate?
Partially. The spadix is protogynous, meaning the female phase finishes before pollen is shed, so a single spadix rarely fertilizes itself. However, plants in cultivation often have overlapping spadices at different stages, and a soft brush moved between them dramatically increases fruit set. Two clones flowering together work even better.
Can I grow Anthurium scandens as a houseplant without a terrarium?
Yes, if your ambient humidity stays above about 55% and you mount or pot it in fast-draining media. It will grow steadily in a bright bathroom or near a humidifier, though fruiting is less predictable than in an enclosed case. Daily misting alone is not enough — humidity needs to be sustained, not pulsed.
How fast does Anthurium scandens grow?
Slowly compared to climbing Philodendron but steadily for an Anthurium. Expect 4–8 new leaves per year on an established plant, with internodes of 2–5 cm. Mounted specimens with consistent moisture and feeding push the upper end of that range.
