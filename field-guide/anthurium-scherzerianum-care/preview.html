Anthurium scherzerianum, the Curled-Spadix Flamingo Flower

Anthurium
1857 Year described from Costa Rica

The original flamingo flower keeps a quieter household than its glossy cousin A. andraeanum, and rewards growers who respect its cloud-forest manners.

Light
Bright indirect; no direct midday sun
Water
When top inch dries; never soggy
Humidity
60–70%, sulks below 50%
Temperature
18–24 °C day, 16–18 °C night
Native range
Costa Rican cloud forest, 1,300–2,100 m
Mature size
30–40 cm tall, slow-growing

Anthurium scherzerianum
Epiphyte · strap leaves · curled spadix

The defining flamingo flower, native to the cloud forests of Costa Rica. Lance-shaped matte leaves on short petioles, with a scarlet spathe and a spiraled orange spadix that no other common Anthurium produces. Slower and smaller than A. andraeanum, topping out around 30–40 cm, which makes it a genuine windowsill plant. Long-lived in a chunky epiphyte mix if you don't drown it.

Common questions

How do I tell Anthurium scherzerianum from Anthurium andraeanum?
Look at the spadix — scherzerianum has a tightly curled, corkscrew spadix, while andraeanum has a straight one. Leaves also differ: scherzerianum has matte, lance-shaped leaves on short petioles, while andraeanum has glossier, heart-shaped leaves on longer stems. The two species also want different conditions, so the ID matters for care.
Why won't my Anthurium scherzerianum flower?
Almost always insufficient light or insufficient night-time cooling. The species flowers best with bright indirect light all day and night temperatures that drop to 16–18 °C. A plant kept in a warm, dim room will hold leaves indefinitely but skip blooming, and no amount of fertilizer fixes that.
Is Anthurium scherzerianum toxic to pets?
Yes — like all Anthuriums it contains insoluble calcium oxalate crystals that cause oral irritation, drooling, and swelling if chewed by cats, dogs, or humans. It is rarely fatal but genuinely unpleasant. Keep it out of reach of pets and children who put leaves in their mouths.
What substrate should I use for Anthurium scherzerianum?
A chunky epiphyte mix, not standard houseplant soil. Roughly 50% medium orchid bark, 25% perlite or pumice, and 25% chopped sphagnum gives the airflow and moisture retention the roots evolved for. The mix should drain freely within seconds of watering.
How often should I repot it?
Every 18–24 months, or whenever the bark has broken down into a fine, dense material that holds water too long. Scherzerianum prefers a snug pot, so step up only one size at a time. Repot in spring when growth resumes, and avoid pulling the root mat apart aggressively.
