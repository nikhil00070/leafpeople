Botanical Tour: The National Tropical Botanical Garden, Kauai, USA — America's Rarest Tropical Plants

In a folded Kauai valley, the National Tropical Botanical Garden has spent six decades pulling rare tropical plants back from the edge — and the collections it keeps will stop any serious grower cold.

The Lawai Valley on Kauai's south shore is one of those landscapes that makes the word 'lush' feel inadequate. A narrow river corridor opens into terraced gardens, the ridgelines pressing close on both sides, the air dense with moisture and the sound of water moving over stone. Standing in Allerton Garden on a clear morning, with Ficus macrophylla buttresses rising ten, twelve feet out of the ground like the flanges of some Gothic ruin, you understand at once why this place was chosen — and why what has been built here over the past six decades matters beyond any single visit.

The National Tropical Botanical Garden was chartered by the United States Congress in 1964, the only such institution in the country with that designation. That charter gave it both a mandate and a responsibility: to research, conserve, and document tropical plants, with particular urgency around species native to Hawaii and the Pacific. What grew from that mandate is a network of five distinct gardens across Kauai, Maui, and Florida — each with its own character, ecology, and collections — centered on the Lawai Valley, where Allerton and McBryde gardens occupy land that has been cultivated, in one form or another, for more than a century.

Common questions

When is the National Tropical Botanical Garden on Kauai open, and do I need to book in advance?
Allerton Garden is open by guided tour only and reservations are strongly recommended, as tours regularly sell out. McBryde Garden is generally self-guided with timed entry tickets available through the NTBG website. Hours and availability vary seasonally, so check the official NTBG site before visiting.
Why is Allerton Garden famous, and what is the Jurassic Park connection?
Allerton Garden is known for its extraordinary designed landscape, integrating formal sculpture and water features with mature tropical plantings in the Lawai Valley. The Diana Fountain area, dominated by enormous buttressed Ficus macrophylla specimens, was used as a filming location for Steven Spielberg's 1993 film Jurassic Park, appearing in the hatching-egg scene.
What makes the National Tropical Botanical Garden scientifically significant?
NTBG was chartered by the U.S. Congress in 1964 and is the country's only federally chartered botanical garden. It maintains living collections and a seed bank focused on rare and endangered Hawaiian and Pacific island plant species, conducting conservation, propagation, and research work on species that have been lost or nearly lost in the wild.
Is the National Tropical Botanical Garden worth visiting if my main interest is aroids and rare tropical plants?
Yes, particularly McBryde and Allerton gardens. Both hold mature tropical collections including significant anthuriums and philodendrons growing in conditions that allow full expression of their growth habits. McBryde's endangered-species collections include Hawaiian endemics rarely encountered anywhere else, and the scale of plants growing in optimal climate conditions is instructive for any serious grower.
