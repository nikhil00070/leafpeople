Botanical Tour: Copenhagen's Palm House, Denmark — Iron, Glass, and a Spiral Into the Canopy

A cast-iron spiral staircase lifts you into the crowns of palms in the heart of Denmark — and into the living logic of Victorian tropical ambition.

Step through the doors of the Palm House on a grey Copenhagen morning and the city disappears. The air thickens, warm and green-scented, and the light changes quality — filtered through glass panels set into a tall iron dome that has been collecting equatorial heat since 1874. Palms press toward the roof. Below them, in the lower registers of the glasshouse, the layered logic of a tropical forest reasserts itself: ferns in the understory, epiphytes clinging to bark, the broad paddles of tropical foliage overlapping in the half-shade. You are standing, technically, in the Østre Anlæg district of central Copenhagen. You feel rather less certain of that than you did a minute ago.

The Palm House anchors the glasshouse complex of the Botanical Garden of the University of Copenhagen — a garden that has occupied its current roughly ten-hectare site since the 1870s and is now administered as part of the Natural History Museum of Denmark. The garden is not a theme park or a contemporary eco-attraction. It is a working scientific collection with deep roots, and the Palm House is its centerpiece: a piece of Victorian engineering that set out to prove a provocative thesis — that the equator could be bottled, shipped north, and kept alive under cast iron and glass in one of Europe's colder capitals.

Common questions

When is the Botanical Garden of the University of Copenhagen open?
The garden's opening hours vary by season, with longer hours in summer and shorter hours in winter, and the garden is generally closed on certain public holidays. Check the Natural History Museum of Denmark's official website for current hours before visiting, as they are updated regularly.
Is the Palm House walkway open to the public?
Yes, the spiral staircase and the rooftop walkway inside the Palm House are accessible to visitors as part of a normal visit to the glasshouses. The walkway offers a view down through the palm canopy and is one of the signature experiences of the site.
Why is the Copenhagen Palm House historically significant?
Opened in 1874, the Palm House is a major example of Victorian cast-iron-and-glass glasshouse architecture, consciously designed in the tradition of Kew's Palm House and the Crystal Palace. It has been in continuous operation since opening and houses palms and cycads that have been cultivated there for well over a century.
Is the Botanical Garden of the University of Copenhagen free to enter?
The outdoor garden grounds are generally free to enter, while the glasshouses may require a ticket. Admission policies can change, so confirm current pricing on the Natural History Museum of Denmark's official website before your visit.
