F1, F2 and Selfing: What Hybrid Generation Labels Really Mean for What You're Buying

Three letters on a listing carry a surprising amount of weight — and a fair amount of fog.

The listing is short and the photo is good: Anthurium papillilaminum × dressleri, F2, $475, shipping Monday. You've been watching this seller for a year. You know the parent cross. You hover on the buy button and then stop on those two characters — F2 — and wonder, with the small private dread of someone who has been burned before, what they actually promise.

The aroid market runs on shorthand. F1, F2, BC, sib, selfed, IBE, RBE — abbreviations borrowed half from formal plant breeding and half from the hobbyist forums of the late 2000s, where growers traded seed and tried to sound rigorous. The labels matter. They also mislead. A serious buyer should know what each one means in textbook terms, what it tends to mean in practice on a Tuesday-night Instagram drop, and where the gap between those two definitions can cost you several hundred dollars.

What the letters mean on paper

In classical genetics, F1 — filial one — is the first generation of offspring from a cross between two distinct parents. If you pollinate a pure Philodendron gloriosum with pollen from a pure P. lynamii, every seed in that berry is, by definition, F1. F2 is what you get when you cross two F1 siblings to each other, or when you self an F1 — pollinate its spadix with its own pollen, assuming it produces fertile pollen and a receptive stigma in the same window, which in aroids it usually does not without intervention.

The reason breeders care about the distinction is Mendel. F1 offspring from two true parents tend to look uniform: the dominant traits show, recessive ones hide, and a tray of seedlings will largely resemble each other. F2 is where the deck reshuffles. Recessives surface. Variance explodes. You see runts and stars in the same flat.

So when a seller writes F1, they are claiming: these are first-generation hybrids from two named parents, and they should be reasonably consistent. When they write F2, they are claiming: these came from crossing two F1s, and they will not be consistent — some will be spectacular and some will be mediocre, and you are buying a ticket in that lottery.

An F1 label is a promise about parentage. An F2 label is a promise about variance — and variance is what you are actually buying.
