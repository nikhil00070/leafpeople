Botanical Tour: The Enid A. Haupt Conservatory, New York, USA — A Crystal Palace of the Tropics

A 90-foot dome of glass and iron rises above the Bronx — and beneath it grow the palms, aroids, and orchids that obsess the collectors who built their own rainforests at home.

Step through the main entrance of the Enid A. Haupt Conservatory on a February morning, when the Bronx outside is the color of a wet parking garage, and the sensory shift is immediate and absolute. The air thickens. The temperature climbs. The light goes green. Above you, a vaulted Palm Dome soars roughly ninety feet to its crown, its white-painted ironwork webbing the glass like the skeleton of something enormous that grew here rather than was built. Below the dome, Sabal and Roystonea palms press toward the glazing, their fronds backlit and faintly luminous. You have walked, in about twelve steps, from a New York winter into a working approximation of the tropical world.

The conservatory opened in 1902, commissioned by the fledgling New York Botanical Garden and built by the celebrated greenhouse firm Lord & Burnham — the same company that shaped American greenhouse architecture across the Gilded Age. The Italian Renaissance inflections in the facade, the colonnaded wings, the symmetry of the eleven interlocking pavilions: all of it signals that this structure was never meant to be merely functional. It was meant to announce something. More than a century later, now bearing the name of the philanthropist Enid A. Haupt, whose generosity rescued it from near-demolition in 1978 and set it on the path to restoration, it still does.

Common questions

How do I get to the Enid A. Haupt Conservatory at the New York Botanical Garden?
The NYBG is located in the Bronx and is directly accessible by Metro-North Railroad from Grand Central Terminal — take the Harlem local line to the Botanical Garden stop, roughly a 20-minute ride. The garden also has parking on site and is reachable by subway with a short walk. Check the NYBG website for current ticketing and hours, as they vary by season.
What is the Enid A. Haupt Conservatory famous for?
The conservatory is considered the grandest Victorian-style glasshouse in the United States. Built by Lord & Burnham and opened in 1902, its central Palm Dome rises approximately 90 feet and anchors eleven interconnected climate galleries. It is a National Historic Landmark and hosts the NYBG's annual Orchid Show each late winter and spring, as well as the popular Holiday Train Show in winter.
What plants can I see in the rainforest galleries?
The tropical and upland rainforest galleries contain palms, aroids including philodendrons and anthuriums, orchids growing in naturalistic mounted conditions, and a layered understory planting that approximates actual forest structure. The cloud-forest house reflects the cool, humid, high-humidity conditions of Andean upland habitats where many collector-favorite species originate.
When is the best time to visit if I want to see the Orchid Show?
The NYBG Orchid Show typically runs in late winter through spring, often beginning in February or March. Exact dates change each year, so check the NYBG's official calendar before planning your visit. If you want to avoid crowds, visits during the Holiday Train Show in November and December tend to be busier; early weekday mornings are the quietest option year-round.
