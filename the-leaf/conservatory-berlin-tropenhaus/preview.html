Botanical Tour: Berlin's Großes Tropenhaus, Germany — One of the Tallest Glass Jungles on Earth

In Dahlem, a steel-and-glass vault built in 1907 holds a full-canopy jungle 26 metres above a Berlin footpath.

Step inside on a grey November morning and the shift is immediate: the cold drops away, the air thickens with moisture, and somewhere overhead — genuinely overhead, four or five storeys up — a canopy of palms closes against the glass. This is the Großes Tropenhaus, the Great Tropical House at the Botanischer Garten Berlin, and the first thing it does is make you recalibrate your sense of scale. Most glasshouses are long and low, a suggestion of the tropics pressed flat under a pane. This one is a vault, an interior sky, and the plants inside it have had a century to believe it.

The garden sits in Dahlem, in the southwest of the city, spreading across roughly 43 hectares of ground that hold something in the order of 20,000 plant species — one of the largest collections on earth by any measure. The Großes Tropenhaus is its architectural and botanical centrepiece, and it was built to do something specific: to let the tropics grow tall.

Common questions

Where is the Botanischer Garten Berlin and how do I get there?
The Botanischer Garten is located in the Dahlem district of southwestern Berlin. It is reachable by S-Bahn to Botanischer Garten station or by U-Bahn toward Rathaus Steglitz. The main entrance is on Königin-Luise-Straße.
Is the Großes Tropenhaus open to the public?
Yes, the Großes Tropenhaus is open to visitors as part of the standard glasshouse complex included in the garden's general admission. Hours vary by season, so confirming current opening times on the garden's official website before visiting is recommended.
What makes the Großes Tropenhaus architecturally significant?
The Tropenhaus, built 1905–1907 and restored 2006–2009, reaches approximately 25 to 26 metres at its peak, making it one of the tallest glasshouses in the world. Its height allows tropical trees, palms, and bamboo to grow to near-natural stature, creating a genuinely multi-storey tropical forest inside a temperate European city.
What plants should I look for as an aroid or epiphyte collector?
The understorey of the Tropenhaus holds climbing aroids including Philodendron and Epipremnum species grown to full maturity on large tree trunks. The complex also includes orchid species, tree ferns, cycads, and the aquatic house with giant Victoria waterlilies. Labels are maintained to current botanical standards.
